The Major Donor Program Health Check

Twelve questions to gauge whether your major and principal gifts program is built to grow — and where the gaps are.

Portfolio & Pipeline

  • 1

    Do you know, today, how many of your donors are capable of a gift at your major-gift threshold?

  • 2

    Is every major-gift-capable donor assigned to a specific relationship manager — not just "the organization"?

  • 3

    Is each gift officer's portfolio sized for real relationship depth? Sector benchmarks commonly cite roughly 100–150 active donors as a workable portfolio — well beyond that, cultivation quality tends to slip.

  • 4

    Do you have a documented pipeline of next-tier donors being cultivated toward major-gift capacity?

Moves & Stewardship

  • 5

    Does every major donor have a documented moves-management plan — a defined next step, not just "stay in touch"?

  • 6

    Are your top donors stewarded on a rhythm that isn't only tied to the next ask?

  • 7

    Do you track donor-centered metrics — retention and upgrade rates, not just total dollars raised? Sector-wide average donor retention has hovered near 43%, with new-donor retention under 20%, so this is worth watching closely.

HNW & UHNW / Planned Giving

  • 8

    Do you have a defined approach for high-net-worth and ultra-high-net-worth donors, distinct from your general major-gifts process?

  • 9

    Is planned or legacy giving actively discussed with your most loyal donors, or is it an afterthought?

Systems & Leadership

  • 10

    Is your donor data clean, current, and actually used to prioritize where staff spend their time?

  • 11

    Does your board understand — and actively participate in — its role in major gift cultivation?

  • 12

    If your most experienced gift officer left tomorrow, would those donor relationships survive the transition?

How to read your answers

Six or more "no" answers signal real room to strengthen your major gifts program before you can reliably scale it. That's not a verdict — it's a starting point for a conversation.

Benchmarks referenced here reflect commonly cited patterns in North American fundraising — including Fundraising Effectiveness Project retention data — not universal rules. Your program's context always comes first.
